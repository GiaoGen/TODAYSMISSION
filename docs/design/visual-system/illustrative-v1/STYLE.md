# TODAYSMISSION Illustrative Visual Standard v1

Status: ACTIVE for new `illustrative-v1` Pack Cover and Mission artwork production.

Primary visual reference: `style-reference.png`

## Core Language

Use a refined hand-drawn 2D picture-book and animation language. Build images primarily from matte color shapes, softened organic contours, and clear readable silhouettes. Add restrained dry-pencil, crayon, or paper grain only where it gives warmth and tactile life.

Characters should feel bright, warm, emotionally legible, and approachable without becoming childish mascots. Preserve intentional asymmetry and small hand-made irregularities. Environments are simplified into purposeful shapes and are generally lighter, quieter, and less heavily outlined than the characters.

Use the reference image for medium, contour behavior, texture restraint, palette relationships, environmental simplification, and overall warmth. Do not copy its exact composition, characters, props, or embedded text.

## Character Authority

The canonical character system controls identity, silhouette, proportions, face, palette, and locked accessories. If the style reference conflicts with a canonical character lock, the character lock wins. Apply the global medium to the canonical identity without redesigning it.

Supporting characters must look drawn by the same visual world. They may be canonical characters or newly designed for a scene, but should not imitate or visually displace the Pack protagonist.

## Composition

Target canvas: `1024 × 1456` portrait.

- Pack Covers use a broader, more environmental view and communicate the Pack-wide behavioral promise.
- Pack Cover typography is generated as part of the complete composition. Its letterforms, color, texture, hierarchy, and placement must belong to the same hand-drawn visual world as the illustration.
- Mission artwork focuses more tightly on the decisive visible action, while retaining enough setting to explain it.
- Across a Pack, vary camera height, angle, depth, subject scale, social geometry, and negative space purposefully.

## Avoid

- glossy 3D, plastic or vinyl surfaces
- anime or manga rendering
- photorealism
- cinematic lens flare, heavy depth of field, or blockbuster color grading
- over-polished concept-art surfaces
- thick equal-weight outlines on every environmental detail
- noisy all-over grain
- unintended lettering, pseudo-text, extra signage, or watermarks
- generic default-font overlays that are visually disconnected from the illustration
- generic mascot poses that erase the behavioral action
