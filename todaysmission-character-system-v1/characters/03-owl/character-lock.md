# TODAYSMISSION Character Lock
## Character 03 — Owl

Status: LOCKED
Version: 1.0
Role: Production Character

---

## 1. Identity

Character 03 is a compact, observant owl belonging to the TODAYSMISSION character universe.

Its identity is defined by:

- ochre-brown rounded body
- large light face-disc / eye-mask region
- large expressive white eyes
- small dark movable pupils
- small simple warm beak
- short rounded wings
- compact bird feet
- soft cream belly
- refined hand-drawn 2D animation rendering

The owl must remain immediately recognizable across poses, scenes, expressions and camera angles.

---

## 2. Character Role

The owl is the observant and curious member of the family.

Core personality:

- observant
- thoughtful
- curious
- insightful
- attentive
- quietly expressive

The owl should feel alert and intelligent without becoming stern or overly serious.

---

## 3. Core Silhouette

The owl has a compact, rounded, low-center-of-gravity body.

Required:

- round main body mass
- very short neck or no visible neck
- small rounded wings
- subtle ear-tuft silhouette
- stable feet
- soft, friendly proportions

Avoid:

- tall slim bird proportions
- realistic owl anatomy
- long wings
- sharp talons
- detailed feathers
- predatory silhouette

---

## 4. Face Disc / Eye Region

The face-disc is a major identity feature.

Required:

- large light cream eye region
- broad, unified mask-like structure
- eyes visually grouped inside the face-disc
- clear contrast against the brown body
- soft rounded geometry

This region must remain distinct from the Dinosaur eye system.

Do not turn it into two isolated eye circles without the broader face-disc structure.

---

## 5. Eyes

The owl uses a high-expression eye system.

Required:

- large white eye areas
- dark movable pupils
- close but not identical spacing
- strong readable gaze
- large pupil travel range
- slight asymmetry allowed

Allowed during acting:

- widening
- squinting
- compression
- one eye higher/lower
- directional gaze
- temporary size variation
- half-lidding
- full closure

Avoid:

- tiny black-dot eyes
- glossy kawaii highlights
- anime irises
- realistic eyes
- eyelashes

---

## 6. Beak

The beak is:

- small
- simple
- warm yellow/orange
- visually secondary to the eyes
- integrated into the lower center of the face-disc

Do not enlarge the beak into a dominant facial feature.

Avoid realistic bird anatomy or detailed nostrils.

---

## 7. Wings

The wings are:

- short
- rounded
- soft
- simplified
- integrated into the body

They may lift, spread, fold or gesture during acting.

Do not let them become:

- long arm-like limbs
- realistic feathered wings
- anatomically detailed flight wings
- human-like hands

When spread, they should still read as soft owl wings.

---

## 8. Belly

The belly is a soft warm cream patch.

It should remain:

- simple
- rounded
- visually secondary to the face-disc
- clean and graphic

Minimal feather marks are allowed.

Avoid dense feather texture.

---

---

## 10. Rendering Language

Use the TODAYSMISSION refined hand-drawn 2D animation style.

Required:

- clean hand-drawn contour
- subtle line-weight variation
- matte color blocks
- restrained dry-pencil texture
- very light cel-like tonal variation
- refined finish level matching the Dinosaur Canonical Model

Avoid:

- raw sketch rendering
- overly fuzzy feather texture
- glossy AI mascot rendering
- perfect vector geometry
- 3D toy aesthetics
- realistic feathers
- plastic materials
- photorealism

---

## 11. Identity Invariants

These properties are LOCKED:

- owl species identity
- ochre-brown rounded body
- large light face-disc
- large expressive white eyes
- dark movable pupils
- small warm beak
- short rounded wings
- cream belly
- compact low-center silhouette

---

## 12. Elastic Acting Zone

These properties are intentionally NOT locked:

- pose
- expression
- pupil direction
- eye openness
- moderate eye size changes
- wing position
- body tilt
- beak opening
- brow/upper-eye acting
- squash and stretch
- sitting / lying / jumping / peeking
- camera angle
- props
- clothing
- accessories

Core principle:

> Preserve identity, not rigid geometry.

---

## 13. Acting Rules

The owl should act primarily through:

- gaze
- pupil direction
- eye openness
- face-disc orientation
- wing placement
- body lean

Strong acting may include:

- wing spreading
- wing lifting
- asymmetrical eye shape
- strong side-eye
- compressed body
- flattened sleeping pose
- peeking
- jumping
- quick shifts in attention

Do not reduce the owl to:

"same neutral body + different facial icon."

---

## 14. Stress-Test Findings

The approved acting stress test confirms identity retention while:

- walking
- running
- jumping
- sitting
- lying down
- sleeping
- peeking
- spreading wings
- reading
- using a phone
- drinking
- eating
- observing
- thinking
- hiding in a scene
- interacting with other characters

Observed acceptable elastic changes:

- face-disc compression while lying down
- temporary eye enlargement
- eye asymmetry
- strong pupil travel
- broad wing gestures

Observed drift to avoid:

- wings becoming too arm-like
- eyes becoming disproportionately huge
- losing the unified face-disc
- excessive feather detail
- face-disc shape changing too radically

---

## 15. Prohibited Drift

Reject generations where the owl:

- loses the large light face-disc
- switches to tiny black-dot eyes
- develops realistic owl anatomy
- gains long wings
- gains sharp talons
- becomes tall or slim
- develops realistic feathers
- becomes glossy 3D
- becomes a plastic mascot
- turns into another bird species
- loses its observant personality

---

## 16. Reference Priority

When generating future artwork, reference priority is:

1. Approved Owl Canonical Model
2. This Character Lock document
3. Approved production artwork
4. Approved Owl Acting Stress Test

The stress test demonstrates allowable elasticity.

It must NOT be treated as a fixed pose library.

---

Character 03 is already designed.

Do NOT ask the model to:

"design a cute owl."

"use Character 03 — Owl exactly as defined by the canonical reference and character lock."

Creative freedom should be spent on:

- acting
- scene
- composition
- props
- clothing
- accessories
- camera
- storytelling

—not on redesigning the character.
