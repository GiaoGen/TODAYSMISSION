# Character and Visual Language

## Character Authority

The seven characters in `todaysmission-character-system-v1` are locked. Use
their reference sheets as the source of truth for:

- silhouette and body proportions;
- species-specific anatomy and features;
- facial architecture and markings;
- base colors and distinctive color relationships;
- recognizable accessories, when canonical;
- rendering language and shape rhythm.

Do not average characters together or let expressive distortion erase their
identity. Expression, pose, squash/stretch, foreshortening, crop, and camera
can be bold when invariant features remain readable.

## Reference Roles and Conflict Resolution

Do not ask one reference image to control everything. Classify inputs before
writing a generation brief:

| Reference role | Controls | Does not automatically control |
|---|---|---|
| canonical character | silhouette, proportions, anatomy, markings, colors, facial architecture | rough edge pixels, sheet layout, pose, background, incidental texture defects |
| character lock text | invariant identity and stated rendering requirements | a fixed pose or camera |
| finish reference | edge integration, material treatment, light, texture restraint, and color hierarchy | character redesign |
| composition reference | framing, balance, scale, support-shape or negative-space ideas | character identity or mandatory props |
| content handoff | behavioral meaning, semantic anchors, safety and confusion boundaries | shot design or literal reenactment |

Know which source controls identity and which controls finish. Record the map
once for a collection and update it only when a source changes; do not repeat
the same reference-role prose for every asset. One source may serve both roles
only when it is genuinely clean enough in both.

Canonical sheets control character identity, not the collection's global edge
finish. Preserve canonical facial architecture and meaningful feature marks,
but do not reproduce heavy sheet contours, rough edge pixels, or a full-object
outline system merely because they appear in the identity reference. This
finish exception does not permit changes to silhouette, proportions, anatomy,
markings, colors, facial architecture, or canonical accessories.

## Integrated Edge Policy

The approved production language is clean and integrated, not mechanically
outlined or mechanically outline-free. Separate most silhouettes and forms
through value, chroma, material change, overlap, occlusion, depth of field,
light, and negative space.

Allow only lines or edge accents that earn a visible job:

- canonical facial marks and fine eye, mouth, brow, nose, or eyelid definition;
- short local structural accents that improve expression, object contact, or
  card-size readability;
- natural highlight, cast-shadow, and contact edges created by coherent light.

Reject a line system that wraps every character or object, produces a sticker
border, mechanically traces silhouettes, or uses the same dark or light weight
on unrelated boundaries. Also reject doubled edges, broken dashes, black
crust, fuzzy halos, scratchy tracing, and narrow edge-following shadow bands
used only as substitute contours.

Texture belongs to the material it describes. Fur, feathers, cloth, paper,
ceramic, foliage, and architecture may use different texture behavior. Keep
texture structurally directed and prevent it from chewing silhouettes,
crossing unrelated boundaries, or becoming uniform noise. Judge finish at card
size first and use 100% inspection only to confirm a visible suspected problem.

## Edge and Texture Finish Contract

Carry the following decisions into the exact image-generation prompt without
changing identity, acting, camera, composition, props, or semantic content:

- request clean integrated boundaries with no heavy uniform or sticker-like
  contour system around characters, props, support shapes, or environments;
- state which canonical facial or local structural lines are permitted and
  keep them fine, purposeful, and subordinate;
- separate forms primarily through value, chroma, material, light, overlap,
  occlusion, depth, and negative space;
- allow deliberate broad tonal modeling, natural edge light, cast shadows, and
  soft atmospheric depth while rejecting glossy/plastic 3D rendering;
- describe the intended species- and material-appropriate texture and prevent
  fuzzy halos, mechanical tracing, texture spill, and uniform noise;
- explicitly exclude visible contour or texture defects in identity references
  rather than reproducing them.

## Palette Relationship

Keep canonical character colors stable and choose the background palette for
scene truth, emotional tone, focal hierarchy, and collection rhythm. Related,
analogous, natural, warm/cool, or complementary relationships are all valid.
Ensure the protagonist separates immediately through value, temperature,
chroma, focus, light, or spatial isolation. Do not force a background into the
protagonist's hue family when a contrasting environment produces a stronger
and more coherent card.

## Tactile Cinematic Storybook Finish

Aim for the finish quality demonstrated by the approved production baseline:
an authored matte storybook illustration with cinematic composition, motivated
light, atmospheric depth, broad tonal transitions, gentle volume, and tactile
species-appropriate materials. Fur may feel soft and directional; other
species and objects require their own material logic rather than copied fur.
Do not equate flat color with quality or remove useful light, space, and
material character merely to simplify compliance. Reject glossy plastic mascot
rendering, generic airbrushed 3D volume, automatic gradient polish, and texture
that ignores form or material.

## Anatomy Topology

Before generation, establish torso orientation, pelvis orientation, shoulder
line, hip line, every limb root, appendage root, and the intended occlusion
order. Trace each arm or foreleg continuously from paw/hand to its own shoulder
and each leg to its own hip; trace every tail from tip to the pelvis. A limb
must never appear to originate from the spine, dorsal plates, neck, belly,
another limb, or an unattached patch of background. For Dinosaur specifically,
dorsal plates remain visibly separate from both arm roots. Foreshortening and
occlusion may hide part of a limb, but the visible geometry must still imply
the correct anatomical attachment.

## Anti-AI Filter

Reject or correct:

- malformed, extra, merged, or duplicated anatomy;
- duplicate cups, tickets, chair legs, table edges, or floating fragments;
- unreadable pseudo-writing, logos, watermarks, or random symbols;
- decorative objects with no semantic or compositional job;
- uniform pseudo-pencil noise or texture sprayed over all surfaces;
- automatic gradients, decorative glows, artificial rim-light effects, or
  vignette effects without a compositional or lighting reason;
- inconsistent perspective, light, contact, scale, or occlusion;
- generic expressions that override character-specific facial structure.

Texture, gradients, and effects are allowed only when deliberately selected
and controlled, never as default polish.
