# Mission Artwork

Mission Artwork is unlocked after completion. Design it as a collectible
reward and a memorable character performance.

## Composition Priorities

1. recognizable character identity;
2. an authored, atmospheric moment that feels worth collecting;
3. one strong acting beat and readable silhouette;
4. card-first composition that survives frontend cropping and overlays;
5. only enough semantic evidence for the selected distance mode;
6. clean color hierarchy and controlled negative space.

The protagonist must own the card, but figure size is a design variable. A
large close character, a medium performance, or a small figure in a wide scene
can all work when focus, light, color, action, framing, or spatial isolation
makes the protagonist unmistakable at card size.

Use as much coherent environment as the visual premise needs. A complete
location may contain several mutually supporting details when they establish
place, atmosphere, scale, or the Mission event. Judge simplicity by hierarchy
and relevance, not by a literal object count: the environment passes when it
forms one story, supports the protagonist, and introduces no competing
narrative. Flat support shapes, grounding shadows, divided fields, shallow
stages, and deep cinematic spaces are all available collection tools.

## Acting

Do not equate social challenge with fear. Never default a completed Mission to
shy, embarrassed, frightened, avoidant, or uncomfortable. Also avoid making
every reward uniformly happy, serene, or victorious.

Choose a precise internal verb for the pose: inspect, commit, brace, glide,
claim, listen, improvise, recover, savor, charge, linger, or another relevant
action. Build face, hands, torso, balance, and crop around the same verb.

## Action and Spatial Logic

For any card containing travel, throwing, rolling, reaching, approaching,
looking toward a target, or cause-and-effect between elements, solve a simple
blocking before detailed generation.

Define:

- **origin:** where the action begins or force is applied;
- **path:** the readable direction through the frame;
- **target:** where the action, gaze, object, or character is going;
- **depth order:** what is nearest, between, and farthest;
- **vanishing direction:** how the environment supports the path;
- **body mechanics:** planted weight, leading limb, counterbalance, torso,
  head, gaze, and any trailing element.

The main action chain should survive as a plain arrow. For a released or
propelled object, the release point, object center, and target should share a
credible trajectory; the object belongs between origin and target in depth.
Motion trails remain behind the moving object relative to its direction of
travel. Gaze, support shapes, floor lines, perspective, and lighting should
reinforce rather than contradict the action.

Strong foreshortening is allowed, but size change alone cannot explain a
contradictory pose or trajectory. If the action reads in the wrong direction
at thumbnail size, change the camera or blocking before rendering details.

Prefer a side, rear-three-quarter, overhead, or other camera that makes the
event immediately legible when a face-forward view would reverse or obscure
the spatial relationship. Showing the full face is not more important than a
coherent action.

## Relevance Test

- Which approved semantic anchor remains?
- Could the card misread as an adjacent Mission or violate a boundary?
- Must the connection read alone, or is Pack context enough for this
  intentionally associative/free card?
- Does removing a prop improve the image without destroying the connection?

Do not add speech bubbles, labels, UI, written instructions, or explanatory
symbols.
